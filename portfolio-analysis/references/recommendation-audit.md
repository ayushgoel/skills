# Recommendation Audit

Use this when reviewing past advice or establishing records for future calls.
Keep personal ledgers, quotes and generated scorecards outside the skill source.

## Preserve the decision made at the time

Select the cohort before looking at outcomes. Define whether “last five” means
five dated reports, distinct ideas or actionable calls. Include all relevant
buys, holds, sells and trims; disclose grouping/ties and any unresolved scope.
Do not omit a failed call, count repeated advice as independent successes or
credit an old thesis with a later revision's evidence.

For each new call record:

- stable ID, original issue timestamp/timezone and available price session;
- identifier, action, buy/sell range or limit, proposed weight/quantity and its
  denominator, order eligibility window and review/expiry conditions;
- confirmed horizon, return hurdle or labeled assumption, earnings basis,
  scenario inputs, thesis, expected catalyst timing and invalidation conditions;
- broad total-return benchmark and a relevant sector/style comparator chosen
  before outcomes, plus an investable passive alternative when practical;
- subsequent revisions with their own timestamps, and separately any known
  actual executions, distributions and costs.

Do not infer exact publication time from a filename or filesystem modification
time. Preserve unavailable historical fields as unknown. A recommendation is
not evidence that the user traded it.

## Measure an executable strategy

Start after the decision became available, using a stated reproducible convention
such as the next eligible session's open. Never award a purchase at a reference
close from before the advice. Apply the original limit and eligibility window.
A daily low touching a limit establishes possible execution, not a confirmed
fill or the intraday sequence. If issue time is unknown, show conditional results
or a conservative later-entry sensitivity rather than assume a favorable fill.
A newly issued weekend call has zero subsequent sessions, not a zero-return win.

Keep these results separate:

- actual user performance, only where dated trades and cash flows support it;
- paper-strategy performance under explicit execution and reinvestment rules;
- reference-price movement, which is descriptive and may be untradeable.

Unfilled orders retain cash or the explicitly selected cash vehicle and its
verified return; include that cash and delay in the strategy denominator. Start its benchmark
when capital was committed to the strategy, not at a later favorable fill. Never
substitute the stock's later rally. For sells/trims, compare the executed or
modeled sale-and-reinvestment path with continued holding, including distributions,
tax/friction where known, and upside forgone. Do not present a risk-reduction
objective as a successful return prediction merely because a risk existed.

## Compare like with like

Align dates, currency, corporate actions, distributions, cash flows and treatment
of fees/tax. Prefer total returns for both assets and benchmarks. If only prices
are verified, label all results price-only and state the limitation. Do not
compare a stock price return with an index total return silently. Use absolute
holding-period returns for very short windows instead of headline annualization.

Report return, benchmark return and excess return in percentage points. With
adequate comparable observations, add drawdown and risk/exposure context. A stock
minus index result is excess return, not demonstrated risk-adjusted alpha.
Consider sector, size, momentum, currency and market exposure before attributing
skill. Do not assert that FII flows caused an individual move from correlation.

For a small-cap challenge, use a preselected diversified small-cap index or an
investable fund known at the original date, with costs/distributions handled
consistently. A named stock actually considered then is a useful additional
comparison, not a substitute for the opportunity set. Do not choose the eventual
best performer or today's constituents with hindsight. Freeze a momentum rule's
lookback and eligibility at the recommendation date if that is the requested
alternative.

## Judge process and outcome separately

Audit source quality, normalization, horizon/catalyst consistency, valuation,
execution feasibility, sizing and decision changes against information available
then. These process defects can be identified immediately. A sound decision may
lose money, and a weak decision may benefit from a broad rally.

Short-window outcomes are provisional. One rally, overlapping positions and a
few calls cannot establish repeatable stock-picking skill. Completing the planned
holding period helps assess the thesis but does not by itself establish skill;
that needs enough independent decisions and exposure/risk-aware evidence across
market conditions. Do not invent a minimum number of months that guarantees it.

Lead the concise scorecard with the verdict, then show the original action,
execution status, comparable outcome/excess return and process finding. State
what evidence changed, what was missed and the concrete correction. Preserve
original calls and link reproducible inputs; do not rewrite them to improve the
track record.
