# Reference Documents

Use when readers need to look something up: vocabulary, data contracts,
catalogues, source indexes, or related-document collections. Optimize for
finding and interpreting the relevant item rather than reading a narrative.

## Make Information Unambiguous

- Define the scope and the authority of the reference. Distinguish an agreed
  definition or contract from a proposed one.
- Preserve exact identifiers and technical names. Explain overloaded terms,
  aliases, and differences between related concepts instead of silently choosing
  one meaning for every source.
- Use examples where definitions alone are insufficient. A consistent example
  across transformations can show how concepts relate; label simplifications
  so omitted fields do not look like a real contract.
- Organize terms, interfaces, or documents by how the reader will find them.
  Tables, diagrams, and prose are options, not mandatory containers.

## Keep Sources And Ownership Clear

- Link to the authoritative contract or original document. Give enough context
  for a reader to know why to open it, without copying its full contents.
- For a document index, choose groupings and metadata that help navigation.
  Include owner, status, or recency when useful and supported, rather than
  requiring the same columns for every collection.
- Date freshness checks when reporting them. A checked date is not a guarantee
  that the source remains current, and an old edit date alone does not prove it
  is obsolete.
- Preserve ownership and source locations. Curating an index does not require
  moving other teams' documents or adopting responsibility for their content.
