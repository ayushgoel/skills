# Decision Registers

Use for a broad view of decisions across a project or domain. Help readers find
what is settled, what remains open, who owns a decision, and where its rationale
lives. A register summarizes many decisions; a
[decision record](decision-records.md) explains one in depth.

## Make The Set Easy To Scan

- Use a compact table with a distinct entry for each decision. Choose columns
  for the register's purpose, commonly an identifier, decision summary, status,
  owner or decision maker, date, and a link to the fuller record or source.
- Preserve identifiers and cross-references used by other pages. Group entries
  by area, status, or chronology when that helps readers navigate the set.
- Keep summaries meaningful on their own, but link to detailed rationale,
  alternatives, and consequences rather than copying them into every row.
- Make pending, accepted, rejected, and superseded decisions distinguishable.
  Use consistent status labels or lozenges. Priority or impact and implementation
  progress are separate facts from decision approval.

## Preserve Traceability

- Record dates and authority from evidence. Do not substitute the date an entry
  was added for the date a decision was made, or invent an owner for an open item.
- Retain the relationship between a superseded decision and its replacement so
  readers can follow how the project changed.
- Reconcile an updated entry with its source within the requested scope. Surface
  conflicting approval evidence rather than silently marking a decision accepted.
- Keep the register focused on decisions. Link execution work to its owning
  tracker, and leave historical meeting notes faithful to what happened then.
