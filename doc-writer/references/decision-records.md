# Decision Records

Use for a focused account of one decision: what it means, why it was made, who
made it, and what follows from it. Follow established ADR or LDR conventions where
useful. A record can be drafted before approval, so its title alone does not
establish that the decision is accepted.

## Preserve The Decision

- Make the decision and its scope easy to find. Explain why it was made and
  what changes for the people or systems affected.
- Preserve the source, decision date, decision makers, and approval state when
  known. The date a record is written or edited can differ from the decision
  date, and review of the wording can differ from approval of the decision.
- Record tradeoffs, rejected alternatives, and objections when the evidence
  supports them. Do not invent a deliberation history to fill a template.
- Separate the agreed rule from proposed implementation, future aspirations,
  and unanswered questions. A settled decision may still leave execution details
  open. If status and source evidence conflict, expose the discrepancy rather
  than silently declaring approval.
- Explain conditions for revisiting a decision when relevant. Preserve history
  when a later decision supersedes it and link the two records.

Keep this page concentrated on the decision and its reasoning. Link to the
owning requirements or design for detailed execution, and to the
[decision register](decision-registers.md) for the wider set of project decisions.
