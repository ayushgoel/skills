# Reviews And Assessments

Use for a written review of a proposal or an assessment of complexity, effort,
risk, or readiness. Help the reader understand the judgment and what evidence
or change would affect it.

## Make The Judgment Traceable

- Identify the artifact or scope reviewed and the evidence it relies on. State
  the overall assessment with appropriate confidence.
- Separate points supported by evidence from recommendations and unanswered
  questions. Recognize sound parts of the proposal when that clarifies which
  changes matter; do not manufacture praise or a quota of criticisms.
- Tie each substantive concern to the relevant behavior or section, its impact,
  and a useful correction or question. Use tables or prose according to what
  makes the relationship easiest to follow.
- Keep proposed changes distinguishable from decisions already made by the
  owning group. Do not turn review suggestions into accepted requirements.

## Match The Assessment To The Claim

- Evaluate effort over the scope being estimated. Coding effort, elapsed time,
  release latency, integration work, and cross-team dependencies can differ.
  Explain which drives the conclusion rather than equating a small diff with a
  small delivery effort.
- Support ratings or estimates with concrete evidence and state material
  assumptions. Preserve ranges when uncertainty is real.
- Identify conditions that would change the assessment, such as an answered
  question, a measured baseline, or a completed integration.
- End with the action or decision the assessment supports when one is needed.
  A review does not itself approve the proposal or authorize changes to it.
