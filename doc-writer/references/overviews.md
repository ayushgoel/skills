# Overviews And Introductions

Use to orient readers and explain a system or topic at a useful level. Choose
terminology, depth, and examples for the audience. Use the separate
[principles](principles-documents.md) or [north star](north-star-documents.md)
guidance when the page establishes project constraints or presents its vision.

## Give The Reader A Coherent View

- Make the purpose and intended outcome clear. Use concrete examples when they
  make an unfamiliar system easier to understand.
- Distinguish what exists, what is committed, and what is aspirational. Keep
  milestone qualifiers close to claims so a target state cannot read as shipped
  capability or a delivery promise.
- Explain the boundaries and relationships that prevent misunderstanding. Link
  readers to the owning requirements, decisions, design, or work plan when they
  need more detail.
- Keep inventories and execution tracking elsewhere when they distract from
  direction. A compact index can help an entry page guide readers without
  copying the underlying documents.
- A short explanation, diagram, FAQ, or spoken script may help, but multiple
  versions and prescribed speaking lengths are not required.
- Source baselines, targets, and effort claims. Make proposals and unresolved
  alignments visible without allowing them to overwhelm the explanation.
