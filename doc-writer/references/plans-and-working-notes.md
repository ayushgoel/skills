# Plans And Working Notes

Use for work plans, coordination proposals, progress updates, meeting notes,
and planning-session records. Match the shape to whether the reader is carrying
out work, checking progress, or recovering what happened in a discussion.

## Support The Next Useful Action

- Make the outcome or workstream clear. Surface actions, dependencies, blockers,
  and decisions needed where they help the reader act.
- Include owners, targets, and completion evidence when known. Distinguish an
  estimate, a proposed assignment, and a commitment; leave missing facts visible.
- Reuse an existing work log or tracker when it owns execution. Link to it from
  narrative updates instead of maintaining competing action tables.
- Keep active plans and task lists current and lean. Remove stale or completed
  execution detail from active work lists while retaining useful completion
  evidence and unresolved questions. Preserve historical context in meeting
  notes and decision records.
- For a coordination proposal, distinguish proposed changes from actions already
  completed and identify whose agreement is still needed. Writing the proposal
  does not authorize the actions it describes.

## Preserve The Meaning Over Time

- For meeting notes, retain the date and useful discussion context. Separate
  decisions, actions, and unresolved questions from suggestions or brainstorming.
  Use chronological notes or a thematic summary according to the intended use.
- For recurring notes, preserve the difference between historical discussion and
  current status. Later evidence may close a question without changing what the
  earlier meeting recorded.
- For progress updates, distinguish completed work, ongoing work, and blockers.
  Give enough evidence to support status without repeating the full work plan.
- Carry forward unresolved follow-ups without inventing answers or silently
  treating them as done. Link durable decisions to the relevant record when one
  exists, rather than leaving important rationale only in meeting history.
