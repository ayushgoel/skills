# Catalyst-Growth Watchlist Framework

Use this framework for volatile companies whose value depends on a visible catalyst:
order-book conversion, capacity commissioning, product-mix change, exports, regulation,
turnaround, deleveraging, or a new business reaching scale. These are not automatically
low-quality businesses, but their evidence is less mature than a proven compounder's.

## 1. Surface candidates

Screen for a combination of operating change and a credible runway, not price momentum.
Seek at least two of the following, supported by dated evidence:

- order intake or executable backlog growing faster than revenue;
- funded capacity due to commission within the investment horizon;
- product, customer, geography, or export mix shifting toward higher margins;
- utilization, margin, ROCE, or cash conversion inflecting from a depressed base;
- industry demand, import substitution, regulation, or technology adoption creating a
  measurable addressable-market expansion;
- deleveraging or working-capital normalization that can turn accounting profit into
  distributable cash;
- promoter or insider capital allocation aligned with minority shareholders.

Reject or downgrade candidates where the story depends primarily on stock-price strength,
an unfinanced capex plan, non-binding orders, repeated equity dilution, related-party
transactions, unexplained receivables, or a peak-cycle annualized quarter.

### Backlog and execution quality

Reconcile headline backlog with firm purchase orders, letters of award, framework
agreements, MOUs and optional tranches. Record related-party and external orders
separately, including cancellation rights, customer financing/readiness, deposits,
collection terms and likely conversion dates where disclosed. Do not count an MOU
again when it becomes a firm order or assume all stated capacity converts to revenue.

Separate equipment supply, limited-scope EPC, turnkey delivery and recurring service
revenue. The same ordered capacity can carry different revenue, margin and working-
capital economics. Test a mix-change thesis against receivables, unbilled revenue,
inventory, supplier finance and cash collections, not just adjusted EBITDA. Compare
current guidance with earlier dated guidance and actual delivery without treating one
timing miss as an automatic thesis failure.

## 2. Classify the evidence state

Use a state machine rather than a binary buy/sell label:

1. **Discover** — plausible runway, but primary-source baseline is incomplete.
2. **Validate** — thesis and KPIs are defined; await operating proof or a suitable price.
3. **Starter** — a deliberately small research position; upside is meaningful and
   permanent-loss risks are understood, but one or more critical KPIs remain unproven.
4. **Scale/Add** — valuation clears the hurdle and repeated results confirm the thesis.
5. **Hold** — thesis and valuation remain acceptable; do not trade ordinary volatility.
6. **Trim** — valuation or position size outruns supported outcomes, while the business
   thesis remains intact.
7. **Exit/Archive** — the thesis is falsified, governance or financing becomes
   unacceptable, or the opportunity cost is compelling after tax.

Never label a company `Exit` merely because a trailing multiple is high. First normalize
earnings using all results available by the as-of date. Conversely, do not call a stock
cheap merely because one strong quarter lowers annualized P/E.

## 3. Build the dashboard

For every `Validate`, `Starter`, or `Scale/Add` name, record:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Thesis | The causal chain from catalyst to per-share cash earnings |
| Baseline | Revenue, normalized EPS, margin, net debt, cash conversion, share count |
| Runway | Addressable demand, capacity, firm versus optional backlog, related-party share and realistic conversion period |
| Proof KPIs | Three to six company-specific operating measures |
| Next catalyst | Dated result, commissioning, approval, order conversion, or deleveraging event |
| Valuation | Bear/base/bull normalized earnings and conservative exit valuation |
| Entry/add zone | Price that clears the hurdle with a risk buffer; never a chart level alone |
| Scale gate | Evidence required before increasing beyond starter size |
| Trim gate | Price/valuation/position-size condition that makes odds unattractive |
| Hard invalidators | Observable facts that break the thesis |
| Position cap | Maximum weight before and after operating/cash-flow proof |
| Review | Last-reviewed date, next review date, and primary-source links |

## 4. Entry and scaling discipline

Enter only when both business evidence and valuation are adequate. Two valid paths are:

- **Price comes to value:** the thesis remains intact while price enters the risk-adjusted
  buy zone.
- **Earnings catch up:** reported results lift normalized earnings and the supported entry
  value while the share price stays broadly stable.

Avoid waiting for every uncertainty to disappear; that often transfers all upside
to the seller. Repeated confirmation is a scaling condition rather than a blanket
ban on a starter before the next result. Size evidence maturity:

- `Starter`: a small research position whose bear-case loss and order size fit the
  user's budget and risk capacity; state the allocation denominator explicitly.
- `Scale/Add`: normally after two consecutive reporting periods confirm the critical
  operating KPI and at least one cash-conversion or balance-sheet KPI.
- Set a maximum only after liquidity, governance, funding, correlated exposures
  and bear-case loss have been assessed.

Use smaller caps for binary regulation, customer concentration, leverage, pledging
or weak liquidity. Portfolio-analysis owns the final weight in a combined portfolio.

## 5. Monitoring and alert logic

Review after every result and material exchange filing. Track direction versus the written
baseline, not merely management guidance.

Use company-specific thresholds, but always cover:

- revenue and per-share earnings versus the scenario path;
- backlog quality, cancellations, execution, and book-to-bill where relevant;
- margin and product mix, separating structural change from project timing;
- operating cash flow, receivable days, contract assets/unbilled revenue, inventory;
- capex progress, utilization, funding, net debt, dilution, and interest coverage;
- customer concentration, exports, regulation, and competitive response;
- promoter transactions, encumbrance/pledge, auditor changes, and related parties.

Treat a single miss as an alert when timing can explain it. Require two consecutive misses
before changing state unless the event is a hard invalidator, such as fraud, covenant stress,
material order cancellation, unexpected dilution, or a sharp increase in promoter pledge.

## 6. Valuation and exit discipline

Model a range rather than a single target. For each scenario, show normalized EPS or cash
flow, the valuation multiple, implied return, and what must go right. Include dilution and
funding needs explicitly.

- **Add:** base return clears the hurdle with a risk buffer and scale gates are met.
- **Hold:** base return is acceptable or evidence is improving, but the add zone is not met.
- **Trim:** price implies the bull case, the position breaches its cap, or a generous exit
  multiple is required despite intact operations.
- **Exit:** hard invalidator occurs, funding/governance risk becomes unacceptable, or the
  revised bear/base distribution indicates permanent loss rather than ordinary volatility.

Document the decision before the next price move. This prevents both momentum chasing and
selling a sound thesis simply because the ticker is volatile.
